# Dataset Information: NASA GEOS Aerosols (2025)

## Overview

This dataset contains high-resolution atmospheric modeling visualizations generated by NASA's Goddard Earth Observing System (GEOS). The data tracks the global transport of **aerosols**—tiny solid or liquid particles suspended in the atmosphere.

The visualizations represent a snapshot of Earth's atmosphere on **August 19, 2025 (20:00 UTC)**. The GEOS model integrates satellite observations, ground (in situ) measurements, and approximately one million hourly weather observations to create these global analyses.

## Data Type

* **Model:** GEOS-FP 2Km Replay (GEOS Forward Processing 2Km Replay)

* **Visualized Variables:** Aerosol Optical Thickness / Concentration by type.

* **Format:** The images are visual maps showing the dispersion and concentration of different particulate matter across the globe.

## The Legend: Color Mapping for AI Image Analysis

To successfully analyze images from this dataset, use the following exact color mappings to classify the atmospheric phenomena present in the frames.

* **BLUE (Sea Salt)**

  * **Description:** Represents sea salt kicked up by strong winds blowing across the ocean.

  * **Visual Patterns:** Often appears as swirling, circular patterns over oceans, representing storms, tropical cyclones, or hurricanes (e.g., Hurricane Erin in the Atlantic). Pale blue to white colors reflect stormy conditions with thick sea salt concentrations.

* **PINK / MAGENTA (Dust)**

  * **Description:** Represents desert dust.

  * **Visual Patterns:** Typically seen as massive hazy plumes traveling across continents and oceans. A primary example in this dataset is Saharan desert dust traveling westward across the Atlantic Ocean toward the Caribbean and the United States.

* **ORANGE / RED (Smoke)**

  * **Description:** Represents organic and black carbon, primarily from burning biomass.

  * **Visual Patterns:** Appears as dense, drifting plumes originating from landmasses. Key sources include wildfires (e.g., raging fires in Spain) and seasonal agricultural burning (e.g., in southern Africa drifting over the Atlantic).

* **GREEN (Sulfates)**

  * **Description:** Represents sulfate particles, which originate from both human pollution (burning fossil fuels from power plants, vehicles) and natural volcanic activity.

  * **Visual Patterns:** Can appear as narrow, concentrated streams (like volcanic plumes from Hawaii's Kilauea or Italy's Mount Etna) or broad regional smog/haze over highly industrialized areas.

## Analytical Context for AI Models

When evaluating an image from this dataset, consider how these colors interact. The atmosphere is a highly interconnected system; it is common to see Pink/Magenta (Dust) interacting with Blue (Sea Salt / Cyclones), or Orange/Red (Smoke) traveling thousands of miles from its origin point due to global wind currents. Density of the color correlates to the thickness (Aerosol Optical Thickness) of the particulate layer.

## Sonification Strategy: "Particulate Polyphony"

Unlike standard temperature maps that use a single continuous colorbar (scalar data), this aerosol visualization encodes **four distinct categorical variables** overlapping in physical space. A successful sonification approach should treat this as a "multi-track stem mixer" where each aerosol type represents a unique instrumental voice or texture.

### Audio-to-Data Mapping Technique

To avoid the "wind-chime noise" effect of directly mapping raw pixels to random pitches, map the *physical characteristics* of the aerosols to distinct synthesis techniques:

1.  **Dust (Pink/Magenta) -> Granular Synthesis:** Dust is composed of physical grains. Map the pixel density/brightness of the pink channel to the *grain density* and *emission rate* of a granular synthesizer. A heavy Saharan dust plume sounds like a thick, textural rustling or crackle.
2.  **Sea Salt & Cyclones (Blue) -> Filtered Noise & Rhythmic Cadence:** Map blue intensity to the volume of a filtered noise generator (representing ocean spray). For swirling hurricane features (vorticity), tie the rotational speed to a rhythmic LFO (Low-Frequency Oscillator) or a traditional drum cadence (like a Sri Lankan *Geta Bera* rhythm) that accelerates as the storm tightens.
3.  **Wildfire Smoke (Orange/Red) -> Harmonic Tension/Distortion:** Smoke signifies destruction or hazard. Map the red channel's intensity to the modulation index of an FM (Frequency Modulation) synthesizer, or introduce dissonance/detuning ($\Delta f$). As smoke density increases, the sound becomes more abrasive and tense.
4.  **Sulfates (Green) -> Continuous Drone/Shepard Tone:** Since sulfates often form continuous streams from volcanoes or industrial centers, map the green channel to a sustained, pure drone or a Shepard tone that rises in pitch as concentration increases.
5.  **Wind Currents -> Spatial Panning:** Use Dense Optical Flow on the overall frame to calculate wind vectors. Map the horizontal flow to 3D binaural panning (HRTF) so the listener can hear the dust and smoke literally "blowing" from left to right across their headphones.

### Data Processing Suggestions

To effectively drive this sonification engine, the processing pipeline must isolate the variables:

*   **Color Masking (Isolation):** Instead of converting the frame to grayscale, use Python (OpenCV) to convert frames to HSV (Hue, Saturation, Value) color space. Create four distinct binary masks based on Hue ranges for Blue, Pink, Orange/Red, and Green.
*   **Density Calculation:** Once isolated, calculate the average brightness (Value channel in HSV) within specific regional grid squares (e.g., a 16x9 grid). This gives you the "Aerosol Optical Thickness" for each specific particle type in that region.
*   **Vector Extraction (Optical Flow):** Run a Dense Optical Flow algorithm (like Farnebäck) *only* on the isolated masks to determine the speed and direction of the smoke or dust plumes, independent of the static background map.
*   **Export as Telemetry:** Export this parsed data as a lightweight time-series JSON file (e.g., `[{"time": 0, "dust_density": 0.4, "smoke_density": 0.8, "wind_vector": [0.2, -0.1]}, ...]`). This allows your browser-based Web Audio UI to play the sound smoothly at 60 FPS without the heavy burden of real-time video processing.
